# [2026-09-04] decision | Execution decisions for the first real hop; the `@other-team/*` question resolved as a check, not a scope expansion

Graham asked the gating question before porting anything: **will NOT bumping the three `@other-team/*` packages to 18 break the guinea-pig hop?** Answering it first required admitting what the rehearsals do not cover — **those packages were stripped from the legacy shells entirely** (private, unresolvable from the public registry) and there are **zero `@other-team` tarballs in the transfer pool**. Every prior claim of "estate-shaped" rehearsal excludes them. Verified in-repo before answering rather than reasoning from the packet's summary.

**The answer: probably a peer-range wall, definitely not a reason to expand scope.** The *mechanism* is observed, not theorized — `ng update` refuses when an installed package declares a peer range the target violates, exactly as `jest-preset-angular@14.1.0` blocked 18→19 at the plan stage. The *input* is one line only the island can read (`npm view @other-team/core-web-angular@1.6.0 peerDependencies`). Three outcomes, tabled in the new plan doc: `^17` peers → `--force` + `--legacy-peer-deps`; Angular as a regular dependency → **worse**, nested duplicate Angular breaks DI at runtime while building cleanly (`npm ls @angular/core` printing >1 version is a hard stop); no Angular → proceed. `core-common` and `core-node` are expected irrelevant; only `core-web-angular` is in question.

**Why not expand scope:** three projects × five rungs plus another team's schedule converts a half-day experiment into a multi-week programme with an external blocker. **No bundle change is needed under any outcome** — those packages come from Nexus, not our pool, so their package.jsons would not change a single tarball we port. And whether the bypass path works cleanly is itself among the most valuable findings available, since it applies identically to all 10+ apps. The limit was named rather than glossed: a peer bypass is proven only by **running** the app, so the run carries a hard acceptance criterion (load a screen rendering `@other-team/core-web-angular` components). `[UNVERIFIED]` — the partial-Ivy linker's forward compatibility is the framework's documented contract, but nobody has run these three through it.

Also corrected a premise of Graham's: `core-web-angular` pinning `@angular/cli 17.0.9` against the estate's 17.3.12 is **irrelevant to consumers** (a library's own devDependencies do not participate in downstream resolution; only its peers reach you). What the skew does indicate is that the other team's upgrade cadence lags this one — a real coordination cost under ADR-005, logged for that planning, not a factor in this hop.

**Graham's execution decisions, now the plan of record** (`first-app-hop-01/island_execution_plan_v1.md`): (1) **port the full 17→22 ladder in one transfer** — endorsed; one transfer cycle is the scarce resource and bytes are not, so this removes the possibility of ever queuing another for toolchain reasons; (2) **skip `--delta-from`** — getting a Nexus listing off the island costs days-to-weeks of approvals, far more than the bytes are worth, and `409 Conflict` is already counted as success; (3) **stage the Nexus uploads one rung at a time** — endorsed for a better reason than convenience: it keeps the registry matched to where the estate actually is, so a loose `^` range in an unrelated app cannot silently resolve higher on its next install.

New doc `island_execution_plan_v1.md` is the spine (Phases A–D, then repeat per rung); the pre-flight checklist, field procedure (new STOP conditions **F-11** peer wall / **F-12** duplicate Angular) and field-notes template were updated to match.
